### Information and consent (online study)

**What this is.** A research study on how a text-based agent responds to short messages. It takes about **18 minutes** on your own computer: 12 short exchanges, a few ratings after each, and one questionnaire at the end. You need a webcam and a quiet place.

**What you will do.** For each exchange you write a short message about how you feel or about your day — by typing, or by speaking it if you prefer — and your webcam takes a few frames. You then tell us how you actually feel, read the agent's reply, and rate it. On two of the twelve turns you will be asked to recall a recent small frustration, or something you look forward to, while writing.

**What is recorded.** Your typed messages, the emotion you report feeling, your ratings, and emotion labels the software estimates. **Webcam frames are sent encrypted to the research server, used only to estimate a facial-expression label, and discarded immediately; they are never stored, viewed by a person, or shared.** If you choose to speak instead of type, the recording is sent for transcription and discarded in the same way — only the words, which you can edit before sending, are kept. Your microphone is never switched on unless you press the speak button. Your typed text (not the frames) is sent to a cloud language-model service (Groq) to produce the reply; please avoid names of other people or details you would not share with an online service.

**Be aware.** The agent's replies are generated in several different ways during the session. Some replies are deliberately based on an inaccurate guess about how you feel, so they may seem off; this is part of the study and we explain the details at the end. If recalling a frustration is uncomfortable you can write about something neutral instead, and you may close the page at any time without penalty.

**Your data.** Stored under a participant number only, on an encrypted research server, for up to 3 years, and used only in aggregate. After the debrief you can delete your data with one click.

**Who to contact.** Researcher: Sainadth Pagadala. Participation is voluntary and has no effect on any course grade; instructors do not see who took part.
